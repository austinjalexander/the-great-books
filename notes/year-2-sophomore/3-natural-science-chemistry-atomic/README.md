# 3-Natural-Science — Chemistry and Atomic Theory

Aristotle on generation and corruption; Aquinas on the principles of nature and the combination of elements; the chemical revolution (Lavoisier, Avogadro, Dalton, Gay-Lussac, Pascal on gases); the longer nineteenth-century atomic story in assembled papers.

**Syllabus citation** (Sophomore, Natural Sciences): Aristotle — *On Generation and Corruption*; Aquinas — *On the Principles of Nature*, *On the Combination of the Elements*; Lavoisier — *Elements of Chemistry*; Avogadro — *Masses and Proportions of Elementary Molecules*; Dalton — *Proportion of Gases in the Atmosphere*; Gay-Lussac — *Combination of Gaseous Substances*; Pascal — *Treatise on the Weight of the Mass of Air*; scientific papers of Berthollet, Couper, Lavoisier, Mendeleev, Richter, Wollaston, Cannizzaro, *et alia*; Atomic Theory Manual.

**Primary editions**: Barnes Aristotle; Aquinas Institute for the short works; Dover reprints for Lavoisier, Dalton; translated anthologies (e.g. *A Source Book in Chemistry*) for the 19th-century papers. See [`RESOURCES.md`](../../../RESOURCES.md).
