# 4-Mathematics — Ancient and Early-Modern Astronomy

The Mathematics track becomes astronomical. Plato's *Timaeus* as cosmogony; Ptolemy's *Almagest* as the high point of geocentric geometric astronomy; Copernicus's heliocentric reframing; Apollonius on conics; Kepler uniting the program in ellipses and the three laws; Archimedes *On Conoids and Spheroids* for the underlying mathematics.

**Syllabus citation** (Sophomore, Mathematics): Plato — *Timaeus*; Ptolemy — *Almagest*; Copernicus — *Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres*; Apollonius — *On Conic Sections*; Kepler — *Epitome of Copernican Astronomy*, *Astronomia Nova*; Archimedes — *On Conoids and Spheroids*.

**Primary editions**: Hackett or Cornford for *Timaeus*; Toomer for Ptolemy; Rosen or Wallis for Copernicus; Green Lion (Densmore or Heath) for Apollonius; Donahue and Wallis for Kepler; Heath for Archimedes.
