# 3-Natural-Science — Life and Measurement

Life sciences come first: Aristotle on the parts of animals; Harvey on circulation; Galen on the natural faculties; Linnaeus on taxonomy; Fabre's entomological observations; Mendel's first pass on inheritance. Hydrostatics from Archimedes and Pascal supplies the initial quantitative physics. The TAC Measurements Manual trains observation.

**Syllabus citation** (Freshman, Natural Sciences): Aristotle — *Parts of Animals*; DeKoninck — *The Lifeless World of Biology*; Fabre — *Souvenirs Entomologiques*; Galen — *On the Natural Faculties*; Harvey — *On the Motion of the Heart and Blood in Animals*; Linnaeus — *Systema Naturae*; Pascal — *On the Equilibrium of Liquids*; Archimedes — *On Floating Bodies*; Mendel — *Plant Hybridization*; papers of Driesch, Gould and Marler, Tinbergen, Goethe, Virchow, von Frisch, *et alia*; Measurements Manual.

**Primary editions**: Barnes Aristotle; Green Lion Press for Archimedes; Mottelay / Dover for nineteenth-century science reprints. See [`RESOURCES.md`](../../../RESOURCES.md#aristotle).
