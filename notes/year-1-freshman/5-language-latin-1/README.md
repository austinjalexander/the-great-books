# 5-Language — Latin I and English Composition

The first half of the Latin sequence and the only formal English-grammar track in the program. TAC's stem method is not a conventional grammar course; the primer teaches morphology through the root, and the student reads graded Latin passages from the start.

**Syllabus citation** (Freshman, Language): Nesfield — *Aids to the Study and Composition of English*; A Primer in Latin Morphology According to the "Stem Method"; Latin Readings According to the "Stem Method" (Freshman Year).

**Primary materials**: Nesfield scans on the Internet Archive; TAC stem-method primer (house material). For alternatives and supplements see [`RESOURCES.md`](../../../RESOURCES.md#latin--learning-the-language).

## Reading order

1. `01-nesfield-aids-to-study-and-composition/` — English grammar, style, arrangement.
2. `02-latin-primer-stems/` — morphology lessons, progressively.
3. `03-freshman-latin-readings/` — graded passages from the stem-method corpus.

Each module in this track uses the humanities three-file layout (`README.md`, `notes.md`, `reflection.md`), with an additional `exercises.md` for Latin drill. Use [`../../../templates/latin-exercise-template.md`](../../../templates/latin-exercise-template.md) to scaffold `exercises.md`.

## Companion readings across the program

- 2-Language — Sophomore Latin: harder passages from Horace, Cicero, Aquinas, Martin of Denmark, and the Canon of the Mass.
- 2-Theology — Augustine and Anselm are standardly read in English but a Latin passage per week keeps the original alive.
- 3-Theology and 4-Theology — Aquinas's *Summa* in English-translation default, with key Latin passages (articles on law, being, the Trinity) parsed alongside.

## Discipline

- Morphology drills every day, even ten minutes is better than none.
- Never paraphrase a Latin passage you have not parsed first.
- Keep a running Latin-English vocabulary list in [`../../../great/common/`](../../../great/common/) or in an Anki deck you own separately.
