# 6-Seminar — Greek Poetry, History, and Philosophy

Seminar is the heart of a TAC year: twice-weekly, evening, discussion-driven. Freshman seminar is Greek from end to end — Homer, the tragedians, Plato, the historians, Aristotle on poetry and rhetoric, Aristophanes.

**Syllabus citation** (Freshman, Seminar): Homer — *Iliad*, *Odyssey*; Plato — *Ion*, *Symposium*, *Republic*; Aeschylus — *Agamemnon*, *Libation Bearers*, *Eumenides*; Sophocles — *Oedipus Tyrannus*, *Oedipus at Colonus*, *Antigone*; Herodotus — *Histories*; Plutarch — *Lives* (Lycurgus, Pericles, Aristides, Alcibiades, Alexander); Aristotle — *Poetics*, *Rhetoric*; Euripides — *Hippolytus*; Thucydides — *History of the Peloponnesian War*; Aristophanes — *The Birds*, *The Clouds*.

**Primary editions**: Lattimore and Fagles for Homer; Chicago "Complete Greek Tragedies" series; Landmark editions of Herodotus and Thucydides; Dryden for Plutarch; Hackett for the Plato dialogues. See [`RESOURCES.md`](../../../RESOURCES.md).

## Discussion discipline

- Every module's `discussion.md` has at least five questions and at least one strong objection, per [`.cursor/rules/module-structure.mdc`](../../../.cursor/rules/module-structure.mdc).
- Cite Stephanus pages (Plato), Bekker columns (Aristotle), or line numbers (poetry, tragedy). Page numbers alone are insufficient.
- Candidate thesis questions land in [`../../../thesis/question-candidates.md`](../../../thesis/question-candidates.md) as soon as they surface.

## Companion readings across the program

- 1-Philosophy — Plato dialogues and Aristotle's *Organon* read in parallel with seminar.
- 1-Natural-Science — Aristotle *Parts of Animals* alongside the *Poetics*.
- 2-Seminar — Virgil, Lucretius, Cicero, and the medievals inherit the framework established here.
