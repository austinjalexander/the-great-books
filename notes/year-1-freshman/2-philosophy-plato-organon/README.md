# 2-Philosophy — Plato and the Organon

The opening of philosophy itself: Plato's dialogues on virtue, knowledge, and the soul; Porphyry's *Isagoge* as the medieval gateway; Aristotle's logical corpus (the *Organon*); Aquinas's short *Proemium* to the *Posterior Analytics* commentary.

**Syllabus citation** (Freshman, Philosophy): Plato — *Meno*, *Protagoras*, *Gorgias*, *Apology*, *Crito*, *Phaedo*; Porphyry — *On the Predicaments* (*Isagoge*); Aristotle — *Categories*, *On Interpretation*, *Prior Analytics*, *Posterior Analytics*, *Topics*; St. Thomas Aquinas — *Proemium to the Commentary on the Posterior Analytics*.

**Primary editions**: Hackett dialogues for Plato; Oxford Clarendon Aristotle for the *Organon*; Aquinas Institute for the *Proemium*. Full list in [`RESOURCES.md`](../../../RESOURCES.md#plato).

## Reading order

1. `01-plato-meno/` — virtue, knowledge, recollection.
2. `02-plato-protagoras/` — the unity of the virtues.
3. `03-plato-gorgias/` — rhetoric and the just life.
4. `04-plato-apology/` — the philosophical life on trial.
5. `05-plato-crito/` — obedience to the laws.
6. `06-plato-phaedo/` — the immortality of the soul.
7. `07-porphyry-isagoge/` — the five predicables.
8. `08-aristotle-categories/` — ten categories of being.
9. `09-aristotle-on-interpretation/` — proposition, truth, modality.
10. `10-aristotle-prior-analytics/` — the syllogism.
11. `11-aristotle-posterior-analytics/` — demonstrative knowledge.
12. `12-aristotle-topics/` — dialectical reasoning.
13. `13-aquinas-proemium-posterior-analytics/` — the division of the liberal arts and the role of logic.

## Companion readings across the program

- 1-Seminar — Homer, Aeschylus, Sophocles provide the pre-philosophical literary backdrop the dialogues respond to.
- 1-Seminar — Plato *Ion*, *Symposium*, *Republic* appear in seminar in parallel with the philosophy dialogues.
- 2-Philosophy (Sophomore) — Aristotle *Physics* and *De Anima* build on the *Organon*'s framework.
- 3-Philosophy (Junior) — *Nicomachean Ethics* and *Politics* assume the student can read Aristotle in this style.
