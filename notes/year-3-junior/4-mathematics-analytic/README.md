# 4-Mathematics — Analytic Geometry and the Beginnings of Calculus

Viète's formalization of algebra; Descartes's *Geometry* as the new analytic method; Archimedes's *Quadrature of the Parabola* as the ancient infinitesimal; selected seventeenth- and eighteenth-century papers on the calculus.

**Syllabus citation** (Junior, Mathematics): Viète — *Standard Enumeration of Geometric Results*, *Introduction to the Analytic Art*; Descartes — *Geometry*; Archimedes — *Quadrature of the Parabola*; selected papers of Hippocrates, Archimedes, Cavalieri, Pascal, Leibniz, Bernoulli, Newton, Berkeley, Bolzano, *et alia*.

**Primary editions**: Witmer translation of Viète (Kent State); Smith & Latham (Dover) facsimile + translation of Descartes's *Geometry*; Heath for Archimedes; Struik's *A Source Book in Mathematics* anthologizes most of the seventeenth-century papers. See [`RESOURCES.md`](../../../RESOURCES.md#the-tac-mathematics-sequence--more).

## Reading order

1. `01-viete-analytic-art/` — both short works as a cluster.
2. `02-descartes-geometry/`
3. `03-archimedes-quadrature-of-parabola/`
4. `04-calculus-papers/` — Hippocrates (Ionian antiphairesis), Archimedes, Cavalieri, Pascal, Leibniz, Bernoulli, Newton, Berkeley's *Analyst*, Bolzano.

## Companion readings across the program

- 1-Mathematics — Euclid (all of it).
- 2-Mathematics — Apollonius *Conics* is the ancient object Descartes is re-describing.
- 3-Natural-Science — Newton's *Principia* uses this mathematics throughout.
- 4-Mathematics — Dedekind on number-theory foundations; Lobachevski on non-Euclidean geometry; Einstein on relativity.
