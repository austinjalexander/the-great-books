# 6-Seminar — Early Modern Political Philosophy, Literature, and Epistemology

The longest seminar year. Cervantes; Aquinas on kingship; Machiavelli; ten Shakespeare plays and the *Sonnets*; Luther, Cajetan, Trent; the modern scientific method (Bacon, Descartes, Pascal); the political philosophers (Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau); Milton, Corneille, Racine; the empiricists (Berkeley, Hume); Swift, Gibbon, Leibniz; Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason* (first read); the American founding documents.

## Reading order

Roughly the order listed in [`../../../docs/CURRICULUM.md`](../../../docs/CURRICULUM.md) — Cervantes; Aquinas *On Kingship*; Machiavelli; Shakespeare (plays in Henriad + tragedy order, then late plays, then Sonnets); Reformation (Luther, Cajetan, Trent); new science (Bacon, Descartes × 3, Pascal); political philosophy (Hobbes, Spinoza, Milton); French classical drama (Corneille, Racine); empiricism (Locke × 2, Berkeley, Hume); prose of the period (Swift, Gibbon, Leibniz, Rousseau × 2); Kant; American founding documents.

Each work gets its own module, numbered `01-` through `NN-`. For Shakespeare, consider grouping related plays (Henriad together) where the reading order and discussion naturally cluster.

## Companion readings across the program

- 1-Seminar — Plato, Plutarch, Thucydides are always alive in early-modern political thought.
- 2-Seminar — Aeneas / Rome / Augustine / Dante feed into Shakespeare and Milton.
- 3-Philosophy — Aristotle *Ethics* and *Politics* are the target of most early-modern political dissent.
- 3-Theology — the *Summa* is the target of Luther and the Reformers.
- 4-Seminar — Kant, Hegel, Marx, and the moderns continue this story.
