# Study Plan

**Current agreement:** no personal weekly budget or track has been selected. Begin with a small session from the [current progress pointer](PROGRESS.md#current-learning-session--2026-10-25); confirm availability before assigning recurring work. These estimates do not add hours to another learning project.

Two pacings for the same curriculum. Pick the one that matches the hours you can realistically put in per week; switch between them as life changes.

- **TAC pace (~4 years)**: ~30 teaching weeks/year at ~25 hrs/week. Feasible if study is your primary occupation.
- **Comfortable (~8 years)**: half-pace at ~12 hrs/week. Feasible alongside full-time work.

Hours below are *total hours for the subject track across one TAC year* — reading, rereading, discussion preparation, Latin drill, demonstrations, and composition. They track how a real TAC year is weighted, not what a single book "deserves" on its own.

Unlike a STEM major, TAC runs **all subjects concurrently within a year**. Do not finish Freshman Philosophy before starting Freshman Seminar — the works are arranged so that, e.g., Plato's *Republic* in seminar and Aristotle's *Organon* in philosophy illuminate each other in the same week.

Each table row names the subject track and its folder. The primary edition / translation for each work lives in [`RESOURCES.md`](RESOURCES.md). The work-level ordering within a track lives in [`docs/CURRICULUM.md`](docs/CURRICULUM.md).

## Operating rules

1. **Concurrency, not serialization.** Do not finish one track before starting another within a year. Rotate through Theology / Philosophy / Natural Science / Mathematics / Language / Seminar every week.
2. **Twice-through for seminar works.** Read once to follow the plot or argument; read again pen-in-hand to mark passages. Do not attempt discussion after a single read.
3. **Passage-citation always.** Every paraphrase in `notes.md` and `discussion.md` cites by book / chapter / Bekker / Stephanus / line number. "Aristotle says" without a citation does not count.
4. **Demonstrate, don't recite.** In Mathematics and Natural Science, reproduce the demonstration on paper before claiming to understand it.
5. **Write.** Compositions in Years 1–3 are non-optional. The thesis thread in [`thesis/`](thesis/) is alive from the first semester, not just Senior year.
6. **Discussion is the core skill.** Every work ends with at least five substantive questions in `discussion.md`, at least one of which is a strong objection to the author's position.
7. **Review cycles.** Begin sessions with brief retrieval from prior work. Proposed longer reviews: every 4 weeks, revisit last month’s strongest seminar text; every 12 weeks, revisit the oldest subject-track notes. Adopt dates within the learner’s actual budget; these are not scheduled reminders.

## Milestones

- **End of Freshman year**: can reconstruct a Socratic dialogue, execute a Euclidean demonstration from Book I, decline a Latin noun, and discuss *Iliad* I without consulting notes.
- **End of Sophomore year**: can explain the four causes in Aristotle's own vocabulary, reproduce Ptolemy's epicycle for a planet, and write a thesis-driven essay on *Confessions* or *Inferno*.
- **End of Junior year**: can argue both sides of Locke vs. Aquinas on the ends of government, follow Newton's *Principia* Book I, and take a decided position on one of your three thesis candidates.
- **End of Senior year**: can defend a 60-page thesis orally, read Einstein's *Relativity* and place it against Newton, and locate any of the ~200 works in the curriculum on a timeline from memory.
