# Senior Thesis — Defense Preparation

> The ten questions the student most expects the committee to ask, each with a short answer tied to a specific passage in the thesis. This is the night-before document; by the morning of the defense, the student should know the thesis well enough to answer any question from it on the spot, without memorized answers.

## Procedure

1. After the final draft, identify the ten questions the committee is most likely to ask. Favor questions that:
   - Probe the thesis statement itself.
   - Probe the weakest-evidence passage in the argument.
   - Push the strongest objection harder than your Section III pushes it.
   - Ask how your position relates to a major author or work in the program you did not foreground.
   - Ask you to reread a specific passage and explain why your reading is preferable.
2. For each, write a short answer (3–6 sentences) with a citation to the exact page / section of your thesis that answers it, plus a citation to the primary text.
3. Rehearse under time pressure: the committee will not let you monologue; answers should be focused and pass the conversation back.
