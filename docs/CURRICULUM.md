# Curriculum

This repository is a self-study replica of the **Thomas Aquinas College (TAC) Great Books curriculum**. The authoritative source is the [TAC syllabus](https://www.thomasaquinas.edu/a-liberating-education/syllabus); every bullet below cites the work exactly as it appears there.

This is the cleanest way to think about the program:

**Goal 1:** read the Great Books cover-to-cover in the order that lets each one illuminate the next.
**Goal 2:** learn to discuss — to examine a text, pose and defend a question, hear an objection, and reply with evidence from the page.
**Goal 3:** write — five essays Freshman year, four Sophomore year, two longer essays Junior year, a Senior Thesis defended before a faculty-style committee.

A **thesis-thread** runs through all four years. Questions begin accumulating in Freshman seminar; one is refined and answered in the Senior Thesis.

## How the curriculum is organized

TAC teaches seven concurrent subjects per year, not a serial "one course then the next" like a STEM major. In this repo:

- Each **year** (Freshman / Sophomore / Junior / Senior) is a top-level phase.
- Each **subject** within a year (Theology, Philosophy, Natural Science, Mathematics, Language, Music, Seminar) is a course.
- Each **work or tightly linked cluster** is a numbered module under its subject (e.g. `01-euclid-book-1`, `03-plato-republic`).
- A **work** is checked off in `PROGRESS.md` only after its reading notes, discussion questions, reflection, and — for mathematical or natural-science works — worked demonstrations are all in place.

Subject tracks that cross years (Latin runs Freshman + Sophomore; Aristotle's *Physics* appears in both Sophomore Philosophy and again in Senior Philosophy; Plato and Aquinas recur throughout) are explicitly cross-referenced in each module's README.

## What "done" looks like

You are at the level of a strong TAC graduate when you can:

- Read a primary-source text closely, cite passages by book / chapter / line or Bekker / Stephanus number, and reconstruct its argument.
- Raise a serious objection to any position you hold and frame a reply that draws on the text.
- Carry out a Euclidean demonstration or a Ptolemaic computation by hand.
- Read a mathematical or natural-science primary source (Newton, Kepler, Maxwell, Dedekind) and follow the argument.
- Read and compose Latin prose at the level of Aquinas's selections and the Canon of the Mass.
- Write a thesis-driven essay with evidence, analysis, objection, and reply.
- Frame a substantial question, research it across the curriculum, and defend your answer orally before a committee.

## The order to learn it in

TAC teaches all subjects **concurrently within a year**, not sequentially. Within a year, the canonical weekly rhythm is roughly:

- Seminar twice a week, evenings.
- Each other subject meets three or four times a week.
- Compositions spaced out, not crammed at term-end.

In self-study, preserve concurrency: do not try to finish "all of Freshman Philosophy" before touching "Freshman Seminar." The works are arranged to illuminate each other *in the same week*.
